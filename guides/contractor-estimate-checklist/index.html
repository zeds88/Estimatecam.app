Practical field guide

A contractor estimate checklist before you send

A useful estimate gives the customer a clear picture of the proposed work and gives you a record of what you priced. Before sending it, check that someone unfamiliar with the site can follow the scope, quantities and assumptions.

1. Confirm the job and the reader

Check the customer name, job location, estimate date and contact details. Date each revision clearly so an older copy cannot easily be mistaken for the current proposal.

Confirm the intended recipient. Remove private internal notes and unnecessary photos.

2. Describe the work in concrete tasks

Replace broad labels such as “landscape improvements” with tasks the customer can recognize. Identify the work area, what you will remove or install and how the area will be left when the work is complete.

  • Does each task have a clear location and purpose?
  • Are preparation, access, removal and cleanup addressed where relevant?
  • Can the customer distinguish included work from possible additional work?
  • Are unresolved conditions clearly identified?

Use photos to support the description. The jobsite photo report guide explains how to connect an overview, detail and observation without assuming a cause.

3. Check quantities, units and arithmetic

For every priced item, confirm the quantity, unit and rate you intended to use. Distinguish a measured quantity from an allowance or an assumption. Do not let a rough field note look like a confirmed measurement.

Recalculate line totals and the final total. Check for duplicated tasks, missing items and inconsistent units. If you include an adjustment or allowance, label it clearly enough that the customer can understand its effect.

4. Make assumptions and exclusions visible

Write down the conditions your estimate depends on: available access, the work area observed or a measurement still awaiting confirmation. Explain what is outside the current scope and what would require another discussion.

Keep these notes beside the relevant work instead of hiding every qualification in a long closing paragraph. Include the payment and scheduling terms you actually intend to offer, and review them before sharing.

A small fictional example

For a walkway refresh, an estimate might separate lifting existing pavers, preparing the observed area, resetting the pavers and cleanup. It identifies which dimensions were measured and flags concealed conditions as unresolved. Replacement materials are either explicitly included or clearly outside the proposed work.

5. Review the exported copy

Read the customer-facing report from beginning to end. Check photo captions, scope, quantities, totals and open questions together.
